Statutory Insolvency Framework – IBC 2016, Regulation 27 & Companies Act Sec 247

The enactment of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 fundamentally transformed India’s corporate distress resolution ecosystem from a debtor-in-possession system to a creditor-in-control paradigm. Under this rigorous legal architecture, asset valuation ceases to be a discretionary commercial estimate; it becomes a statutory condition precedent that dictates the rights, recovery thresholds, and voting protocols of secured financial creditors, unsecured financial creditors, and operational creditors alike.

Pursuant to Regulation 27 of the IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016, the Resolution Professional (RP) is statutorily mandated within forty-seven (47) days of the insolvency commencement date to appoint two Registered Valuers for each class of assets owned by the Corporate Debtor. These valuers must be registered with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) under Section 247 of the Companies Act, 2013, read alongside the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017. Any appraisal conducted by non-registered entities, unregulated accounting firms, or chartered engineering panels lacking IBBI individual and entity credentials is void ab initio before the Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal).

  • Regulation 27 Dual Valuer Mandate: Strict statutory requirement compelling the RP to appoint two distinct Registered Valuers per asset class. Both valuers operate under sealed, non-disclosure confidentiality covenants to independently calculate Fair Value and Liquidation Value without mutual consultation.
  • Section 247 Companies Act Governance: Mandatory professional accreditation under IBBI and recognized Registered Valuers Organisations (RVOs). Strict conflict-of-interest prohibitions ensure complete independence from the Corporate Debtor, financial creditors, and prospective resolution applicants.
  • Section 30(2)(b) Distribution Floor: Liquidation Value calculated under Regulation 35 establishes the statutory minimum recovery floor. Dissenting financial creditors and operational creditors cannot receive less than the liquidation value they would have been entitled to under Section 53 waterfall priorities.

Fair Value vs. Liquidation Value – Dual Valuation Benchmark Under the Insolvency Code

Regulation 35 of the CIRP Regulations establishes two distinct valuation benchmarks that Registered Valuers must independently compute. Confusing these two metrics or applying non-statutory methodologies represents one of the most frequent grounds for valuation reports being set aside by NCLT benches.

Fair Value (FV) – Regulation 2(1)(hb)

Defined as the estimated realizable value of the assets of the corporate debtor, if they were to be exchanged on the insolvency commencement date between a willing buyer and a willing seller in an arm’s length transaction, after proper marketing and where the parties had each acted knowledgeably, prudently and without compulsion. The standard premise is Highest and Best Use (HABU) under orderly market circumstances using Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), Income Capitalization, and Market Comparable Multiples.

Liquidation Value (LV) – Regulation 2(1)(k)

Defined as the estimated realizable value of the assets of the corporate debtor if the corporate debtor were to be liquidated on the insolvency commencement date. Assumes forced liquidation, piecemeal asset surrender, or distressed slump sale under a severely compressed disposal window (90 to 180 days) under court auction constraints, modeling Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC) and empirical distress discounts.

CIRP Valuation Timeline – From Admission to Resolution

The Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process operates under rigid statutory deadlines established under Section 12 of the IBC (180 days, extendable to 270 days, with a hard outer limit of 330 days inclusive of litigation). Asset valuation must be executed with extreme procedural discipline across a sequential ten-stage workflow:

  1. NCLT Admission & Moratorium Commencement: Section 7, 9, or 10 admission order triggers Section 14 moratorium freezing alienation and claims.
  2. IRP Appointment & Public Announcement: Form A publication, creditor claims collation within 14 days, and Committee of Creditors (CoC) constitution.
  3. Regulation 27 Valuer Appointment: IRP or RP formally appoints two independent IBBI Registered Valuers for each asset class within 47 days of the Insolvency Commencement Date (ICD).
  4. Forensic On-Site Physical Inspections: Multi-disciplinary engineering and surveying teams conduct mandatory physical site audits, GPS boundary validations, machinery condition ratings, capacity verification, and title reconciliation.
  5. Fair Value Econometric Modeling: Valuation based on Highest and Best Use (HABU) principles, discounted cash flows, and unconstrained transaction assumptions.
  6. Liquidation Value Distress Calculation: Valuation under forced liquidation conditions, piecemeal dismantling, market liquidity absorption, and empirical statutory haircuts.
  7. Confidential Submission to Resolution Professional: Password-protected, encrypted delivery directly to the RP under Regulation 35(1) sealed cover.
  8. Committee of Creditors (CoC) Confidential Review: Disclosure under Regulation 35(2) only after receiving compliant resolution plans and securing signed non-disclosure undertakings from CoC members.
  9. Resolution Plan Feasibility & Section 30(2) Compliance: CoC benchmarks applicant proposals against Liquidation Value to verify statutory priority payments for dissenting creditors under Section 30(2)(b).
  10. Resolution Plan Approval or Liquidation Order: 66% CoC approval leads to Section 31 NCLT approval; lack of resolution triggers Section 33 liquidation where Liquidation Value serves as the reserve price benchmark for public auction.

Operational Bottlenecks, Missing Documentation & Third Valuer Situations

Key CIRP bottlenecks include uncooperative suspended management who conceal records or deny physical access (requiring Section 19(2) applications), missing Fixed Asset Registers (FAR) without equipment invoices, and unregistered or encumbered leasehold titles.

Under Regulation 35(1)(b), if the estimates of the two appointed Registered Valuers diverge significantly (exceeding 15% to 25%), the RP must appoint a Third Registered Valuer. The final Fair Value and Liquidation Value are derived by computing the mathematical average of the two closest estimates.

Common Reasons NCLT & IBC Valuation Reports Are Challenged Before Tribunals

  • Arbitrary Distress Haircuts Without Empirical Basis: Applying unsubstantiated blanket haircuts without market comparable evidence violates IBBI standards.
  • Omission of Physical On-Site Asset Inspection: Relying exclusively on book entries or desk reviews without ground verification constitutes a fatal procedural breach.
  • Misclassification of Encumbered vs Unencumbered Assets: Failing to verify charge priorities distorts Section 53 waterfall allocations.
  • Ignoring the 15% Variance Rule: Failing to appoint a Third Valuer under Regulation 35(1)(b) when reports differ significantly invalidates the valuation baseline.
  • Methodological Inconsistency: Valuing viable going-concern manufacturing entities on a scrap basis depresses enterprise value.
  • Disregarding Off-Balance Sheet & Intangible Assets: Omission of high-value patents, mining leases, or brand equity triggers appellate challenges.
  • Deviating from Insolvency Commencement Date (ICD): Using non-statutory valuation reference dates leads to tribunal rejection.
  • Inadequate Disclosure of Environmental Liabilities: Failing to quantify remediation or decommissioning costs skews net realizable calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions on CIRP & NCLT Valuations

1. What is the statutory difference between Fair Value and Liquidation Value under the IBC?

Fair Value (Reg 2(1)(hb)) assumes an arm’s-length exchange between willing parties under highest and best use with adequate marketing. Liquidation Value (Reg 2(1)(k)) models realizable proceeds upon liquidation with forced distress surrender within 90 to 180 days.

2. Why does the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code mandate two registered valuers for each asset class?

Regulation 27 requires twin independent Registered Valuers to ensure cross-validation, procedural objectivity, and creditor protection against systemic valuation errors.

3. What protocol is triggered if there is a significant difference between the two valuation reports?

Under Regulation 35(1)(b), if the two estimates diverge significantly (variance exceeding 15% to 25%), the RP must appoint a Third Registered Valuer. The final baseline is the mathematical average of the two closest estimates.

4. At what stage of the CIRP are valuation figures disclosed to the Committee of Creditors (CoC)?

Under Regulation 35(2), figures are strictly confidential and shared only after compliant resolution plans are received and creditors sign non-disclosure undertakings.

5. Can a resolution plan provide an amount lower than the Liquidation Value to operational creditors?

No. Section 30(2)(b) establishes that operational and dissenting financial creditors cannot receive less than their liquidation value entitlement under Section 53 priorities.

6. How is the valuation of continuous process plants and specialized machinery conducted during CIRP?

Continuous process machinery is evaluated using Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC), remaining useful life (RUL) assessments, obsolescence analysis, and dismantling/scrap realization discounting.

7. What is the role of registered valuers in assessing PUFE transactions?

Registered Valuers quantify the economic loss and undervaluation differential for avoidance applications under Sections 43, 45, 50, and 66 during statutory claw-back look-back periods.

8. What is the typical timeline for completing an IBC valuation report under Regulation 27?

Standard multi-asset valuation reports require 30 to 45 business days, accommodating on-site forensic inspection, data collation, econometric modeling, and pre-issuance review.

9. How are disputed, un-hypothecated, or unregistered assets treated in an IBC valuation report?

All corporate debtor assets are inventoried. Disputed or unregistered assets receive explicit qualification notes and risk-adjusted discounts reflecting title defects or litigation encumbrances.

10. How does the Liquidation Value determined during CIRP impact subsequent liquidation proceedings?

If CIRP fails and liquidation is ordered under Section 33, the established Liquidation Value serves as the statutory reserve price benchmark for public e-auctions under Liquidation Process Regulations.
