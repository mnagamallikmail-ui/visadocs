Methodologies: Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC) & MEA

In accordance with International Valuation Standards (IVS 300) and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules 2017, our engineering valuations deploy the Cost Approach:

  • Modern Equivalent Asset (MEA): We benchmark the cost of replacing the asset with a modern equivalent providing equal utility, accounting for modern technological advantages and energy efficiencies.
  • Physical Deterioration: We calculate effective age vs. design lifespan based on operational run-hours, maintenance logs, refurbishment history, and operating environment corrosiveness.
  • Functional Obsolescence: We quantify capacity mismatches, excess operating costs, and technological obsolescence due to automation advances.
  • Economic & External Obsolescence: We adjust for external market headwinds, environmental emission bans, and industry over-capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions — Plant & Machinery Valuation

What methodologies are deployed for Plant and Machinery Valuation?

We primarily deploy the Cost Approach via the Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC) and Modern Equivalent Asset (MEA) methods, accounting for physical deterioration, functional obsolescence, and economic obsolescence under IVS 300 rules. Where active secondary markets exist, the Market Approach is applied.

Why is an IBBI Registered Valuer required for Plant & Machinery valuation in India?

Under Section 247 of the Companies Act 2013 and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC 2016), any valuation of plant and machinery involving corporate borrowers, mergers, or insolvency resolution must be certified by an IBBI Registered Valuer specializing in the Plant & Machinery asset class.

Do you provide Chartered Engineer Certificates for plant and machinery?

Yes. Our empanelled Chartered Engineers certify Remaining Useful Life (RUL), machinery condition, depreciated replacement cost, and import valuation reports compliant with Indian Customs (SVB) and foreign trade regulations.
